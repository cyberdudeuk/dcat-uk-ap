    Cross-border data flows

    Updated 2 sections on this page are new or updated since 1.2.0-rc.1 (2026-11-06). Everything that changed

    Data moves between the UK nations, to and from the Crown Dependencies and the British Overseas Territories, across the Commonwealth, and with allies and partners. DCAT-AP-UK records where data is held and sent, and on what basis personal data leaves the UK, so the question is answered before anyone asks for the data.

    This is not legal advice. Whether a transfer is restricted, and what covers it, is for the controller to decide with the ICO's guidance. The adequacy column shows UK adequacy regulations (data bridges, under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 since 5 February 2026). It was triangulated against secondary sources on 6 November 2026 (provenance S), because the ICO's list could not be fetched directly. It changes, so check before relying on it.

    Where data goes

    The UK nations

    England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. Data that stays here is not an international transfer, though devolved law may still differ.

    5 on the list

    Crown Dependencies

    Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man are not part of the UK. Each has its own data protection law and UK adequacy regulations cover them.

    3 on the list

    British Overseas Territories

    Fourteen territories under UK sovereignty but outside the UK for data protection. Gibraltar has UK adequacy regulations; for the others, check the safeguard each time.

    14 on the list

    The Commonwealth

    56 member countries, including the UK. Membership says nothing about data protection: check each country.

    55 on the list

    The European Economic Area

    The EU states, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway. Covered by UK adequacy regulations.

    30 on the list

    Other partners

    Countries the UK often shares with. Some have full or partial UK adequacy regulations.

    18 on the list

    How to describe it

    1. Give every place the data is held or sent in "where the data is held or sent", including a supplier's cloud region.
    2. If the data contains personal data and any place is outside the UK, give the transfer safeguard (rule R-08). The validator checks this.
    3. Give the law that applies, as legislation.gov.uk addresses.
    4. Express what may be done with the data as an ODRL usage policy, so a partner's systems can read it.
    5. Keep the transfer risk assessment, if one is needed, with the data owner. The record says what the basis is; it is not the assessment.

    Rule R-08: When the data contains personal data and is held in or sent to anywhere outside the UK, give the transfer safeguard. It is checked by the SHACL shapes, the JSON Schema and the browser validator. See the worked example.

    The properties

    • Where the data is held or sent (dcatuk:jurisdiction): Every country or territory where the data is stored or processed, or to which it is sent.
    • Transfer safeguard (dcatuk:transferMechanism): The basis under UK GDPR on which personal data in the resource may be sent outside the UK.
    • Applicable legislation (dcatap:applicableLegislation): Legislation that requires, governs or permits the publication or sharing of the resource.
    • Usage policy (odrl:hasPolicy): A machine-readable policy, in the W3C Open Digital Rights Language (ODRL), saying what may and may not be done with the resource.
    • Geographic coverage (dct:spatial): The geographic area the data is about.

    Transfer safeguardsUpdated

    As the ICO guidance sets them out. Source: International transfers (guidance updated 15 January 2026)S

    Transfer safeguards
    SafeguardCodeWhat it means
    Not a restricted transferukcl:transfer-mechanism/not-restrictedThe ICO three-step test shows the flow is not a restricted transfer, for example because the receiver is not a separate legal entity, or the data is not personal data in the receiver's hands.
    UK adequacy regulations (data bridge)ukcl:transfer-mechanism/uk-adequacyThe receiver is in a country, territory or international organisation covered in full by UK adequacy regulations, known as data bridges. Since the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 came into force on 5 February 2026, the test is that protection there is not materially lower than in the UK.
    Partial UK adequacy regulationsukcl:transfer-mechanism/uk-adequacy-partialCovered by adequacy regulations for a sector or a class of receiver only, such as organisations certified to the UK-US data bridge.
    International data transfer agreement (IDTA)ukcl:transfer-mechanism/idtaThe ICO international data transfer agreement.
    UK Addendum to the EU standard contractual clausesukcl:transfer-mechanism/addendumThe EU standard contractual clauses with the ICO UK Addendum.
    Binding corporate rulesukcl:transfer-mechanism/bcrBinding corporate rules approved by the ICO, for transfers within a group.
    Instrument between public authoritiesukcl:transfer-mechanism/public-body-instrumentA legally binding and enforceable instrument between public authorities or bodies.
    Administrative arrangement between public bodiesukcl:transfer-mechanism/administrative-arrangementProvisions in an administrative arrangement between public authorities or bodies, authorised by the ICO.
    Approved code of conduct or certificationukcl:transfer-mechanism/code-or-certificationAn approved code of conduct or certification scheme, with binding commitments from the receiver.
    An exception (derogation)ukcl:transfer-mechanism/exceptionOne of the exceptions in Article 49 UK GDPR, such as explicit consent or important reasons of public interest. For occasional transfers only.
    Law enforcement transfer rulesukcl:transfer-mechanism/law-enforcementA transfer by a competent authority under Part 3 of the Data Protection Act 2018, which has its own rules.

    Alliances and partners

    • Five Eyes and defence partners: Security and defence sharing has its own rules and classifications. DCAT-AP-UK describes OFFICIAL data only; records about classified data must not be in an OFFICIAL catalogue. NATO's data-centric reference architecture uses DCAT terms, including usage policies. NATO Data Centric Reference ArchitectureS
    • European Union: DCAT-AP-UK keeps every DCAT-AP mandatory property, so UK records can be harvested by EU portals. The high-value datasets extension adds what EU reporting needs. DCAT-AP 3.0.1V
    • United States: DCAT-US 3.0 is also built on DCAT 3, so shared terms carry over. The crosswalk shows which UK terms have no US equivalent. DCAT-US Schema v3.0S
    • Data spaces: The data space extension follows the Dataspace Protocol, so UK catalogues can offer data to European and international data space participants under machine-readable policies. Eclipse Dataspace ProtocolS

    The crosswalk to DCAT-AP and DCAT-US shows which terms carry over.
