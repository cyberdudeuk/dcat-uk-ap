    Profiles around the world

    A desk scan on 6 November 2026 of DCAT-based profiles at supranational, national and sector level, and of the UK sector standards a pan-UK profile must sit beside. Each was read for what it offers people who must comply: specification, validator, examples, machine-readable shapes and schema, quality scoring, converters, templates and extension mechanisms. It is not an audit: "Not seen" means it was not found in the scan and may exist. No ranking is claimed beyond what each source says itself.

    What DCAT-AP-UK takes from them

    A validator anyone can use without installing anything

    Check records in the browser, nothing uploaded; the same verdict as the published SHACL and JSON Schema, proven on every build.

    Learned from: DCAT-US 3.0, DCAT-AP-NO, DCAT-AP 3.0.1

    A quality score people can act on

    The five quality dimensions and their points, shown live in the record builder and the bulk checker, with what to add next.

    Learned from: data.europa.eu quality assessment, HDR UK Gateway schema, DCAT-AP_IT

    A showroom of complete examples

    Worked examples for every kind of record and every extension, each passing three validators.

    Learned from: DCAT-AP-NO, DCAT-AP-SE

    Conventions that settle how to fill a property

    Each property says what it is for, how it differs from its neighbours, and gives guidance and an example.

    Learned from: DCAT-AP.de, DCAT-AP-SE

    A converter from OpenAPI to a data service record

    Paste an OpenAPI description; get a draft record, checked, with the gaps listed.

    Learned from: DCAT-AP-NO

    A migration route from the old model

    xGov MEM records convert and are checked in one step, with notes on what changed.

    Learned from: DCAT-US 3.0

    Sector extensions on one base

    Nine UK extensions that only raise obligations or add properties, each with its own shapes, schema, example and break.

    Learned from: DCAT-AP 3.0.1, HealthDCAT-AP Release 7, GeoDCAT-AP 3.1.0, MLDCAT-AP 3.x

    High-value datasets rules inside the national profile

    An optional extension for exchange with EU catalogues, which creates no UK obligation.

    Learned from: DCAT-AP-NL 3.0

    Usage policies machines can read

    ODRL policy templates, including whether data may train AI models, and a property that links them.

    Learned from: Dataspace Protocol, Croissant 1.1

    Field-by-field references and a spreadsheet route

    A template with one row per dataset, and a bulk converter and checker in the browser.

    Learned from: DCAT-US 3.0, HDR UK Gateway schema

    Multilingual by design

    Records carry every UK language. A Welsh portal is on the open register (O-07).

    Learned from: DCAT-AP-CH

    What the UK adds that the scan did not find elsewhere

    • A critical data asset switch that turns on the extra properties the Data Asset Management Policy asks for, in the same record.
    • The Government Security Classifications in every record, with SECRET and TOP SECRET kept out of OFFICIAL catalogues.
    • Where data is held and sent, and the UK GDPR transfer safeguard, checked by a rule (R-08).
    • Connection security for the post-quantum inventory, and warnings on weak checksums.
    • Contact forms preferred over mailboxes, and never a named person.
    • Three independent validators held to the same verdict on every example and every deliberate break.
