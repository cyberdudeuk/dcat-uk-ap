    The profile

    Sector extensions

    Updated 4 sections on this page are new or updated since 1.2.0-rc.1 (2026-11-06). Everything that changed

    Extensions add what a sector needs on top of DCAT-AP-UK, as DCAT-AP does for health, geospatial data and statistics. An extension never weakens the base: a record that conforms to an extension also conforms to DCAT-AP-UK.

    How extensions work

    • An extension may raise an obligation (optional or recommended to mandatory), narrow a cardinality, require a pattern, add properties and add classes. The build refuses an extension that would weaken the base.
    • Each has its own SHACL shapes and JSON Schema (the base plus the extension), a worked example and at least one record that must fail. All three validators are held to the same verdict on them on every build.
    • Choose one in the record builder, the record checker or the bulk checker, or pass it to the MCP server.
    • Terms in another project’s namespace are that project’s. Terms this profile adds (dcatuk:) are provisional until reviewed with the sector (open register O-13).

    The extensionsUpdated

    Health and care

    Describes health and care data well enough for a researcher or analyst to judge it before asking: who is covered, how many records, which clinical codes, and how access is controlled.

    NHS bodies in all four nations, the UK Health Security Agency, social care, trusted research environments, health research charities and suppliers.

    Geospatial

    Adds what GeoDCAT-AP and UK GEMINI ask for: coverage, coordinate reference system, resolution, topic category and lineage, so geospatial records can sit in the same catalogue as everything else.

    Mapping agencies, local authorities, environment and marine bodies, utilities, and anyone publishing location data to data.gov.uk or a spatial data infrastructure.

    Official statistics

    Says what status the statistics have under the Code of Practice, and describes their dimensions and measures so tables can be found by what they count, as StatDCAT-AP does.

    The Office for National Statistics, departmental statistics teams, the devolved statistics offices, and any producer of statistics.

    Transport and mobility

    Says which mode of transport, which network and which data standard (TransXChange, NeTEx, SIRI, GTFS, DATEX II) a dataset uses, as mobilityDCAT-AP does for European national access points.

    The Department for Transport, transport authorities, operators, the bus and rail open data services, and suppliers.

    High-value datasets (for EU exchange)

    Adds the two properties the DCAT-AP high-value datasets profile requires, so UK records can be harvested by EU catalogues that report under the EU high-value datasets regulation.

    UK bodies that also publish into EU portals, take part in EU programmes, or whose data EU partners harvest.

    AI-ready

    Makes the record answer the questions an AI team asks first: may we train on it, is it synthetic, where is the data card, what policy applies, how good is it, and what period does it cover.

    Anyone publishing data that may be used to train, fine-tune or evaluate AI models, and anyone building AI systems on public data.

    Data spaces

    Follows the Dataspace Protocol catalogue rules: every dataset carries a machine-readable usage policy, every distribution names the service that gives it, and the catalogue names its participant.

    Organisations taking part in data spaces and connector networks, in the UK or with European and international partners.

    Private and voluntary sector

    GOV.UK organisation addresses only cover government. This extension asks for a registered identifier for every organisation, in the org-id.guide form, so a charity in Scotland, a company in Northern Ireland and a council in Wales are named the same way.

    Companies, charities, social enterprises, mutuals, universities, housing associations and other bodies outside government that share data with it or publish their own.

    Energy

    Records the open data triage outcome for presumed-open energy system data, and asks for the Dublin Core elements the guidance requires. Every Dublin Core element already has a DCAT-AP-UK property.

    Network companies and other parties obliged to follow Ofgem Data Best Practice, energy suppliers, and the energy data community.

    Geodesy

    A candidate module on top of the geospatial extension. It says which reference frame and epoch the coordinates are in, how they were observed, and how uncertain they are, so precise positions are not silently mixed across frames or dates. Requirement RQ-23.

    Geodetic and positioning teams, national mapping and survey bodies, and anyone publishing coordinates, reference frame products or observation time series whose meaning depends on the frame, the epoch and the uncertainty.

    Health and care Proposed

    Describes health and care data well enough for a researcher or analyst to judge it before asking: who is covered, how many records, which clinical codes, and how access is controlled.

    For
    NHS bodies in all four nations, the UK Health Security Agency, social care, trusted research environments, health research charities and suppliers.
    Aligned to
    • HealthDCAT-AP Release 7: HealthDCAT-AP Release 7S
    • HDR UK Gateway dataset schema: HDR UK Gateway schemataS
    Identifier
    https://w3id.org/dcat-ap-uk/ext/health (proposed; does not resolve yet)
    How the UK differs
    • HealthDCAT-AP names a Health Data Access Body under the European Health Data Space. The UK has none, so access is described by the rights statement and the trusted research environment address instead.
    • Counts of people follow the publisher's disclosure control. Round them, and leave them out where disclosure control says so.
    • The NHS national data opt-out applies in England only; say whether it has been applied.

    Geospatial ProposedUpdated

    Adds what GeoDCAT-AP and UK GEMINI ask for: coverage, coordinate reference system, resolution, topic category and lineage, so geospatial records can sit in the same catalogue as everything else.

    For
    Mapping agencies, local authorities, environment and marine bodies, utilities, and anyone publishing location data to data.gov.uk or a spatial data infrastructure.
    Aligned to
    • GeoDCAT-AP 3.1.0: GeoDCAT-AP 3.1.0S
    • UK GEMINI 2.3: UK GEMINI 2.3S
    • MEDIN discovery metadata standard (marine): MEDIN discovery metadata standardS
    Identifier
    https://w3id.org/dcat-ap-uk/ext/geospatial (proposed; does not resolve yet)
    How the UK differs
    • UK GEMINI is encoded in ISO 19139 XML. This extension is the DCAT side; the crosswalk on the adoption page maps the elements.
    • Give the coordinate reference system as a standard the data conforms to, for example http://www.opengis.net/def/crs/EPSG/0/27700 for British National Grid.

    Geodesy CandidateNew

    A candidate module on top of the geospatial extension. It says which reference frame and epoch the coordinates are in, how they were observed, and how uncertain they are, so precise positions are not silently mixed across frames or dates. Requirement RQ-23.

    For
    Geodetic and positioning teams, national mapping and survey bodies, and anyone publishing coordinates, reference frame products or observation time series whose meaning depends on the frame, the epoch and the uncertainty.
    Aligned to
    • GeoDCAT-AP 3.1.0: GeoDCAT-AP 3.1.0S
    Identifier
    https://w3id.org/dcat-ap-uk/ext/geodesy (proposed; does not resolve yet)
    How the UK differs
    • A candidate for discussion with geodetic specialists, not an agreed module. Its terms are in the dcatuk namespace for now; whether modules get namespaces of their own is open (RQ-07).
    • Details of stations and other infrastructure are left out on purpose. They can be sensitive, and belong in a controlled layer if they are needed at all.
    • Give the frame and the vertical reference as standard identifiers, such as EPSG codes, and name a realisation rather than an ensemble. EPSG 9067 is ETRF2000, a realisation of ETRS89; EPSG 4258, the ETRS89 ensemble, is accurate only to about 0.1 metres. EPSG 5701 is ODN height. Codes checked at the EPSG registry on 6 November 2026. The epoch is a decimal year, such as 2025.0.
    What the geodesy extension adds
    PropertyClassObligationTermChangeDefinition
    Geographic coverageDatasetMandatorydct:spatialRaised from recommendedThe geographic area the data is about.
    Reference frameDatasetMandatory Provisionaldcatuk:referenceFrameNewThe coordinate reference system or terrestrial reference frame, and its realisation, that the coordinates are given in.
    Coordinate epochDatasetRecommended Provisionaldcatuk:coordinateEpochNewThe date the coordinates refer to, as a decimal year, for frames in which positions move over time.
    Vertical referenceDatasetOptional Provisionaldcatuk:verticalReferenceNewThe height system the vertical values use, such as a national height datum or the ellipsoid.
    Observation techniqueDatasetRecommended Provisionaldcatuk:observationTechniqueNewHow the positions or heights were observed.
    Horizontal uncertainty (metres)DatasetRecommended Provisionaldcatuk:horizontalUncertaintyNewThe typical horizontal uncertainty of the positions, in metres, at the confidence level given.
    Vertical uncertainty (metres)DatasetOptional Provisionaldcatuk:verticalUncertaintyNewThe typical vertical uncertainty, in metres, at the confidence level given.
    Confidence level (per cent)DatasetRecommended Provisionaldcatuk:confidenceLevelNewThe confidence level the uncertainties are stated at, as a percentage, such as 95.
