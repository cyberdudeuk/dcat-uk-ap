    Adoption toolkit

    Updated 1 section on this page is new or updated since 1.2.0-rc.1 (2026-11-06). Everything that changed

    Everything an organisation needs to adopt DCAT-AP-UK, in any sector: what conformance means, a route for your sector, a checklist, crosswalks from the models you use now, how to be harvested, words for contracts, and how to name organisations outside government.

    Conformance levels

    The record builder, the checker and the bulk checker show the level of every record. The quality score follows the five dimensions of the EU metadata quality assessment. K

    Conformance levels
    LevelTestWhat it means
    Level 1: CoreNo errors.The record can be harvested and found. Every mandatory property is there and every value is valid.
    Level 2: RichNo errors, and every recommended property given.People can judge the data without asking. This is the level to aim for.
    Level 3: ExemplaryLevel 2, and a quality score of 351 or more out of 405.Ready for reuse at scale, including by AI tools and other countries' catalogues.

    A route for your sector

    Central government departments and agencies

    1. Find your critical data assets with the DAM playbooks.
    2. Map your catalogue's fields with the crosswalks.
    3. Export records and check them in bulk.
    4. Switch on the critical data asset flag where it applies.
    5. Declare conformance in your catalogue record.

    Devolved governments

    1. Use the nation in the jurisdiction property and the right languages.
    2. Record Welsh, Gaelic and Irish titles and descriptions as language-tagged values.
    3. Map your national catalogue (for example the spatial data infrastructure of your nation) with the crosswalks.
    4. Harvest to the National Data Library when it is ready.

    Local government

    1. Start with the spreadsheet template; no new software is needed.
    2. Use your GOV.UK organisation address or local authority code for the publisher.
    3. Check records in bulk and fix the errors.
    4. Publish the records as a DCAT catalogue file next to your open data.

    NHS, health and care

    1. Apply the health extension.
    2. Describe access through a trusted research environment in the rights statement.
    3. Apply disclosure control to counts of people.
    4. Map existing HDR UK Gateway records with the crosswalk.

    Private sector and suppliers

    1. Apply the private and voluntary sector extension for your registered identifier.
    2. Make your product export and import DCAT-AP-UK, and pass the test records.
    3. Use the data space extension if you take part in one.
    4. Say which version you conform to.

    Voluntary sector and charities

    1. Use your charity number in the org-id form (for example GB-CHC-1234567, GB-SC-SC012345 or GB-NIC-100000).
    2. Use the spreadsheet template: no technical skills needed.
    3. Say who may use the data, and for what, with a usage policy.
    4. Ask for help with the contact form.

    Research, universities and data services

    1. Give a DOI as another identifier.
    2. Apply the AI-ready extension for data used in machine learning.
    3. Export Croissant for machine learning tools.
    4. Describe cross-border access with jurisdiction and transfer safeguard.

    Being harvested

    1. Publish one catalogue file. A dcat:Catalog with every dataset and data service, as Turtle or JSON-LD, at a stable address such as /catalog.ttl. Give it a modified date.
    2. Page large catalogues. Split catalogues of more than a few thousand records into pages, and link the pages, so harvesters do not time out.
    3. Use stable identifiers. Each record's address must not change when the record is edited; harvesters use it to match records and avoid duplicates.
    4. Declare the profile. Give the catalogue dct:conformsTo with the profile version, and each catalogue record the same.
    5. Serve the right media type. text/turtle for Turtle and application/ld+json for JSON-LD, with cross-origin access allowed so browser tools can read it.
    6. Keep CKAN in step. CKAN sites can use ckanext-dcat to publish the catalogue; map the extra UK fields with scheming, as the CKAN crosswalk shows.

    Naming organisations outside government

    GOV.UK addresses only cover government. For everyone else use a registered identifier in the org-id.guide form; the private and voluntary sector extension checks the form. K
