    Context

    Requirements stream

    New This page is new since 1.2.0-rc.1 (2026-11-06). Everything that changed

    Over the last year the same need has come up in many places: catalogue owners, departments putting the Data Asset Management Policy into practice, national discovery services, defence and security users, policing and geospatial teams. They are not asking for the same standard. They are asking for the same architecture: a common DCAT core, governed specialist layers, clear access and handling semantics, machine-readable validation, provenance that survives harvesting, and mappings that say what they lose.

    How this is evidenced. Requirements come from cross-government engagement and from public sources. Engagement records are not published: people, meetings and documents are withheld, so each requirement says only what kind of community raised it. Public sources are linked and dated. A requirement is met only when a property, rule or test shows it.

    Evidence: E Raised in cross-government engagement. The record is not published.; V Checked against a public primary source on the date given.; O A rule this profile sets for itself.

    A family of profiles, on one core

    From the foundation up, L0 to L4: each level builds on the ones before it, shares one validator, and exists only with its own tests. Layers marked + sit alongside the spine. Reuse before inventing; profiles, not forks; discovery stays separate from access.

    1. L0

      Foundation standards Met

      DCAT 3, DCTERMS, SKOS, PROV-O, DQV and the other W3C and OGC vocabularies every layer builds on.

      See it

    2. L1

      DCAT-AP-UK core Met

      The common spine for UK government catalogues, aligned with DCAT-AP 3.0.1. Required of every catalogue.

      See it · 15 requirements

    3. L2

      Geospatial (GeoDCAT-UK) Partly met

      Location, coordinate reference systems, resolution and lineage, reusing GeoDCAT-AP and mapped to UK GEMINI. Required for geospatial datasets, series and services.

      See it · 2 requirements

    4. L3

      Geodesy module Partly met

      A candidate module for reference frames, epochs, observation techniques and uncertainty, without loading them onto the core.

      See it · 1 requirement

    5. L4

      Controlled partner overlays Controlled

      Defence, security and allied exchange: stricter validation, handling and control semantics, applied only in partner exchanges. Specified, governed and tested outside this public repository.

      Data asset management layer Partly met

      What critical data asset reporting needs: ownership, criticality and provenance.

    7. +

    Design principles

    1. A common core, with governed layers for specialist needs. No single profile carries everything.
    2. Neutral about technology and supplier. Platforms connect through mappings, never by becoming the standard.
    3. Being able to find a record never implies being allowed to have the data.
    4. The record and the data can be handled differently. Each can say how it must be handled.
    5. Provenance survives harvesting and mapping: who said what, and where it came from.
    6. Every rule is machine-readable, and three validators give one verdict.
    7. A mapping says what it loses.
    8. Existing catalogues connect through mappings and adapters, not by being replaced.
    9. Public artefacts never carry controlled semantics. Controlled layers are specified and tested elsewhere.
    10. Built in the open wherever security allows.

    Controlled layers

    Some requirements can only be specified or tested with material that is not public: allied specifications, control-set models and test records for protected environments. This repository holds none of it. It holds the open core and the extension points those layers attach to, and it lists such requirements with status "Controlled" so the gap stays visible. Their detail belongs in a controlled repository, governed by the right authorities.

    What the open core gives those layers to attach to:

    • Namespaced modules, merged by one function, for terms a community owns
    • Record-level and data-level handling, kept apart
    • Explicit missing values with a reason, including withheld
    • Conformance by level and by layer
    • Offline validation with one verdict

      Validated requirements for the core, data asset management, controlled domains, geospatial, platforms and governance, each in its own session.
    5. Produce the minimum viable profile Started · Month 3
      A candidate release with scope, conformance statement, vocabularies, examples and migration guidance from the xGov MEM.
    6. Build validation alongside Started · From month 1
      Shapes, positive and negative test records, mapping tests and loss reports, written with the profile, not after it.
    7. Pilot with real catalogues Planned · Months 3 to 5
      Harvesting, mapping, provenance and validation measured on a representative group of catalogues, including one controlled-domain case under appropriate controls.
    8. Settle the repository and publication model Planned · Before the candidate release
      An organisation-owned public repository for the open core, and controlled repositories for restricted mappings and test records.

    The case against

    This is a list of good intentions, not evidence.

    Every requirement says how it is evidenced and what would show it met. Engagement evidence is labelled E and not overstated: it shows a recurring need across communities, not that any one body has asked for this profile.

    Withholding who raised what makes it impossible to check.

    True from outside. The engagement record exists for the owning team and the governance route; publishing it would aggregate information about protected communities, which the project avoids (ADR 0015).

    A profile family will fragment into many profiles nobody can track.

    Layers share one core and one validator, and a layer exists only with its own tests. The family is smaller than the set of bespoke translations it replaces.

    Security semantics do not belong in a civil profile at all.

    Only the minimum is in the core: handling of the record kept apart from handling of the data, and withheld values. Anything stricter is a controlled layer.
